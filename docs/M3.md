# M3 — Browser teleop console

A human drives the S1 from a browser, through the safety governor, while the
camera streams back. It is also the first honest measurement of the **video path
end to end**: everything in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) §6 measured frames
arriving *in the Go process*, and said nothing about what a viewer actually sees.

## Running it

```bash
./scripts/build.sh

# With the robot: Wi-Fi joined to its AP (docs/M1-RUNBOOK.md)
./bin/s1teleop
open http://localhost:8700

# Without the robot: synthetic video, sink discards. For working on the UI.
./bin/s1teleop -mock
```

| Flag | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `-addr` | `localhost:8700` | Listen address |
| `-fps` | `15` | Browser video rate. Encode is ~23.6 ms/frame, so 30 costs most of a core |
| `-quality` | `70` | JPEG quality |
| `-max-chassis` | `0.60` | Hard ceiling on chassis deflection; the console scales down from it |
| `-max-gimbal` | `0.70` | Hard ceiling on gimbal deflection |
| `-speed-level` | `slow` | Robot-side chassis gear: `slow`, `medium`, `fast` |
| `-deadman` | `250ms` | Stop after this much console silence |
| `-mock` | off | No robot; synthetic video |
| `-wifi-direct` | on | Robot's own AP. Off = router mode |

### Speed: three independent levers

The chassis originally felt sluggish next to the turret because two reductions
were stacked on it and none on the turret. The levers, in order of bluntness:

| Lever | Range | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Robot-side gear** (`ChassisSpeedLevel`) | slow / medium / fast, chassis only | `-speed-level` at launch |
| **Deflection ceiling** | continuous 0–1, per axis group | `-max-chassis`, `-max-gimbal` at launch |
| **Console gear** | 25 / 50 / 75 / 100% of the ceiling | live in the browser, persisted |

The console gear only ever scales **down** from the ceiling: it can ask for
less, never more, so the governor remains the sole authority on limits. That is
why `-speed-level` is a launch flag rather than a button — unlike deflection, it
changes actual metres per second for the same stick position, which is the
operator's call and not the browser's.

`ChassisSpeedLevelCustom` would give per-axis speeds — independent forward,
strafe and rotation limits — but the library marks it *"not supported yet"*.

The blaster is **infrared only** — light and sound, no projectile. `TypeBead` is
not reachable from the console (DECISIONS.md #7). Arming is explicit, expires on
its own, and is refused during an e-stop.

## The cockpit

The console was rebuilt in M3.7 (DECISIONS.md #17). Video is full-bleed;
translucent chips carry frame rate, resolution, gear, battery and link state and
**fade after four seconds** of no input, returning on any keypress or mouse
move. Everything not needed while driving — gears, arming, perception detail,
recovery — lives in a drawer behind <kbd>Tab</kbd>.

Two rules the layout may not break:

- **The e-stop never fades and never moves.** Fixed bottom-right, bound to
  <kbd>Esc</kbd>.
- **A caption always shows its age**, judged against its own tier's cadence
  rather than the absolute scale used for video (#18).

Narration defaults to the **top** of the frame, is draggable, remembers where it
was put, rests to low opacity after nine seconds, and hides with <kbd>N</kbd> —
because at 20 cm off the floor the bottom of the view is the ground you are
about to drive over (#19).

## The drawer, and why `state` is the useful field

`state` is the governor's reason for the current output, reported every tick.
It turns "why won't it move" into an answer:

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ok` / `clamped` | Driving. `clamped` means you asked for more than the limit |
| `no-command` | Nothing has been submitted yet |
| `deadman` | The console went quiet for more than 250 ms |
| `ttl-expired` | The command outlived its own TTL |
| `estop` | Held until explicitly cleared |
| `link-down` | Bridge or telemetry lost |

`browser fps` and `frame age` are measured **in the browser**, by decoding the
MJPEG stream by hand rather than handing it to an `<img>`. That is the number
this milestone exists to produce.

## A finding from bring-up: background tabs stop the robot

Chrome throttles timers in hidden tabs to roughly 1 Hz. A backgrounded console
therefore stops feeding commands, and **the deadman stops the vehicle within
250 ms** — measured at 0.9 Hz command rate with the tab hidden.

This is correct behaviour, not a bug: nobody should be driving a robot from a
tab they cannot see. It is made legible rather than mysterious — the console
watches `visibilitychange`, sends an explicit zero rather than waiting for the
timeout, and shows a banner saying the vehicle stopped and why.

## Verified in mock mode

- UI renders; the hand-written MJPEG parser decodes to canvas correctly
- Browser-measured **15.0 fps**, frame age ~26 ms, 1280×720, matching the
  configured stream rate exactly
- `/stream` delivered 46 JPEG frames in 3 s (~18.7 KB/frame at quality 70)
- Over-range commands are clamped server-side; the console cannot exceed limits
- E-stop refuses subsequent commands, and clearing it does not resurrect them
- Disconnecting the console leaves the deadman to stop the vehicle

## On hardware

Driven against a real vehicle 2026-09-04. The operator's verdict on the control
experience: *"the controls feel perfect… the streaming video feels solid."*

| | |
|---|---|
| Stream to the browser | **~14.8 fps** against 15 configured |
| Frame size | **~55 KB** — real imagery compresses ~3× worse than the test pattern |
| Bandwidth | roughly **6.6 Mbps** at 15 fps |
| Connect to driving | ~2.3 s |

**Two bugs that only a robot could find.** Drive and turret pitch were inverted
because `controller.StickPosition` negates Y and not X — strafe and yaw felt
correct, which is exactly what made it confusing. And Chrome throttles
background-tab timers, so a hidden console trips the deadman.

Neither was reachable from a test suite. That keeps being the lesson with a
bridge that never acknowledges anything (DECISIONS.md #12).
